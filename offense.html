      On defense

      Without snap counts, the best available signal is how often a player's involvement turns into a havoc play (TFL, sack, INT, PBU or forced fumble).

        Success means gaining 50% of the distance on 1st down, 70% on 2nd, and all of it on 3rd or 4th. Touchdowns always count; turnovers never do.

        Explosive plays are runs of 10+ yards and completions of 15+. Red zone is inside the opponent's 20. Opportunities are carries plus charted targets; sacks and passes with no charted receiver are left out.

        Pass efficiency is the high school/NCAA rating: (8.4×yds + 330×TD + 100×comp − 200×INT) ÷ attempts. Havoc plays are TFL + sacks + INT + PBU + forced fumbles.

        Adjusted rate = (successes + 10 × team rate) ÷ (opportunities + 10). Expected success comes from every scrimmage snap this season, both teams, grouped by down (1st, 2nd, 3rd/4th) and distance (1–3, 4–6, 7+). 80% range is a Wilson interval on the raw rate.

        The game charts don't record who was on the field, so defensive numbers are shares of involvement rather than per-snap rates. Small samples swing hard; treat any flag as something to check on film.
